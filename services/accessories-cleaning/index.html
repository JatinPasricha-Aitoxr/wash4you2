What actually happens to a tie

A necktie is not one piece of fabric. It is an outer shell, a wool or cotton interlining that gives the knot its body, a tipping fabric at both ends, and a single slip stitch running the length of the back that lets the whole thing stretch and recover every time you tie it. Put that in water and the layers shrink at different rates. The shell ripples, the interlining twists, and nothing brings it back. So ties go through solvent cleaning, spotted by hand first, then finished over a shaped form so the edges stay softly rolled. A tie that comes back with hard, knife-flat edges has been ironed, not finished.

Caps: check the brim before you hand it over

Press the peak of your cap between finger and thumb. If it feels papery and holds a crease, the stiffener inside is cardboard, and that cap can never be wet washed — the brim buckles and stays buckled. Plastic-stiffened peaks spring back and will take a controlled hand clean. The other thing we look at is the sweatband. That grey-white tide line is two problems in one: salt, which dissolves in water, and sebum, which does not, so a cap needs both an aqueous and a solvent step to shift it. Sweat that has sat through a Gurugram summer oxidises into the cotton and yellows it. We can usually lighten that. We cannot always erase it, and we will tell you which one you are getting before we start.

Scarves, stoles and dupattas

Pashmina, cashmere and fine wool are washed by hand with no agitation and dried flat, because a wet knit stretches under its own weight the moment you hang it. Fringes are combed straight while still damp; left alone they dry into knots that then have to be worked loose dry, which sheds fibre. Block-printed and bandhani dupattas get tested hardest, since a lot of hand-printed dye is unfixed, and one bleed test on a seam allowance is what stands between a beautiful dupatta and a pink haze across the white ground. Real zari is metal wrapped around a thread core: it tarnishes and it hates heat, so anything with zari, gota or sequins is pressed on the reverse, through a cloth, at low temperature.

What cannot be saved

Turmeric on silk usually leaves a yellow ghost — curcumin is a dye, and it bonds to protein fibre. Perfume or attar sprayed straight onto a silk tie or scarf strips colour where it landed, and that pale patch is permanent. So is the faded line along the fold of a scarf that has hung in one position in a wardrobe for years; that is sunlight, not soil. Ink and paan are judged item by item. And if a tie has thinned at the knot after years of the same four-in-hand, cleaning will make that visible. It did not cause it.

Before you hand the item over

Tell us what the stain is and roughly how old it is — that one sentence changes the chemistry we start with. Tell us if you have already tried something at home, because lemon, vinegar, toothpaste and hairspray all leave residues we have to work around. Take off brooches, tie pins and cap badges. Nazuk kapde, extra care — delicate things, handled slowly, and counted with you at the door before they leave.

How it works

From your door and back again

Counted with you at the door

Small items are the easiest to lose, so every accessory is counted and logged in front of you at pickup and acknowledged on a written receipt.

Construction check

We look at what the item is actually made of and how it is built: tie interlining, cap brim stiffener, hidden zari, glued trims, loose stitching. Accessories often carry no useful care label, so this is done by hand and eye.

Colourfastness test

A damp test on a hidden edge or seam allowance before anything else. Hand-block prints, bandhani and deep reds on white grounds are the ones that move.

Spotting, one stain at a time

Stains are worked individually with the right agent for the stain type, not a general pre-soak. Oil, pigment and dye stains each need a different first move, and makeup on a scarf edge is usually two of them at once.

The cleaning method matched to the item

Solvent for ties and structured silk, gentle hand washing for knits and cottons. The item decides, not the queue.

Drying and reshaping

Knits flat, caps shaped so the crown does not collapse, fringes combed out. Nothing hangs while it is heavy with water.

Finishing and separate packing

Low-heat pressing where the item allows it, then packed on its own. Your order never shares a bag with anyone else's.
