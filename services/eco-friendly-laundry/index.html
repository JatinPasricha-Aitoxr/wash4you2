Sorting is most of the job

Almost everything that goes wrong in a wash is decided before the machine starts. We sort your order three ways: by colour, by fabric weight, and by how soiled it is. Colour matters most on anything new. Unwashed dark denim, maroon and deep red cotton, and cheap black tees shed loose dye for the first three or four washes, and once that dye has been through heat it is fixed for good. Tell us if a piece has never been washed and it goes in separately. Fabric weight matters because a bath towel tumbling against a fine cotton shirt will abrade the shirt. Soil level matters because kitchen clothes and gym wear need a longer main wash than a shirt worn for four hours in an air-conditioned office.

Gurugram water, and why whites go grey

Much of Gurugram runs on hard groundwater. Calcium and magnesium react with detergent and leave a film that builds up wash after wash: whites turn a dull grey, towels stiffen and stop absorbing, dark cottons pick up a chalky cast. Adding more detergent makes it worse, because the undissolved detergent is the residue. We dose to the load and the water and run an extra rinse rather than a bigger scoop. If your whites have already greyed over years of home washing we can lift them noticeably, but we cannot return them to day-one white, and anyone promising that is selling optical brightener, which washes straight back out.

What we cannot save

Underarm yellowing on white shirts is aluminium from antiperspirant bonded into the fibre along with body protein. We reduce it; we do not erase it. Bleach marks from toilet cleaner or hair dye splash are missing colour rather than added dirt, and nothing cleans colour back in. A stain that has already been ironed or tumble-dried is usually permanent, because heat sets oil and protein alike. Most common of all: a stain scrubbed hard at home with bar soap and a brush leaves a fuzzy, faded patch where the fibre has broken, and that damage is done before we ever see the garment. So leave the stain alone and tell us what caused it. The treatment for oil is the opposite of the treatment for blood, milk or egg, and hot water on a protein stain sets it permanently.

Before you hand the bag over

Empty every pocket. One ballpoint pen can mark a whole load and one tissue will coat it in lint. Close zips and fasten hooks so they do not snag knitwear. Count your own socks, hankies and innerwear, since small items are what people notice missing. Keep out anything embellished: sequins, mirror work, beaded kurtas, silk, wool and suits belong in dry cleaning, not in a wash-and-fold load.

What comes back

Your order is counted with you at the door, written on a receipt you keep, bagged on its own and washed as its own load. It never shares a drum with another household's clothes. It comes back dried at a controlled temperature, folded and packed. Wash and fold means clean and neatly folded, not pressed. If you want crisp collars and sharp trousers, ask for wash with steam ironing instead. One last thing: don't leave the sealed pack in a damp cupboard through the monsoon. Open it and let the clothes breathe.

What we take

Items we handle

  • Shirts, t-shirts and everyday topsCotton, linen and cotton blends worn for work or at home. Collars and cuffs get a separate pre-treat before the main wash, because that is where body oil and Gurugram dust collect first.
  • Trousers, jeans and shortsDenim is washed inside out and cool to slow fading. New dark jeans go in on their own for the first few washes so the indigo does not travel.
  • Cotton kurtas, salwars and everyday ethnic wearPlain and lightly printed cotton and cotton-blend ethnic wear. Anything with zari, sequins, mirror work or a silk lining is moved to dry cleaning instead, and we will tell you if we spot it at sorting.
  • Towels, bedsheets and pillow coversWashed apart from clothing because towel lint transfers onto everything else. Towels get an extra rinse and no fabric softener, since softener is what leaves them water-repellent and flat.
  • Innerwear, socks and hankiesSmall items are the ones that go missing, so they are washed in mesh and counted back against the pickup receipt. Count them yourself before handover too.
  • Gym, sports and activewearSynthetic performance fabric holds odour in the fibre rather than on it. It needs a cool wash and a proper rinse; hot water and heavy softener are what make gym clothes smell again the moment you sweat.
  • School uniforms and children's clothesFrequent, gentle washing rather than aggressive chemistry, so name tapes, printed logos and elastic survive the term.

How it works

From your door and back again

Pickup and count at your door

Every piece is counted with you before the bag leaves. You keep a written acknowledgement of exactly what was collected. Weighing, where the order is charged by weight, is done dry and in front of you, never after washing.

Inspection and stain flagging

Each piece is checked for existing damage, loose buttons, open seams, thin patches and stains. If we think a stain will not fully come out, or a garment is too fragile for a wash-and-fold load, we tell you before we start rather than after.

Sorting

Split by colour, by fabric weight and by soil level. New or unwashed dark colours are isolated. Towels are kept away from clothing to stop lint transfer.

Wash

Your order runs as its own load and is never combined with another customer's clothes. Detergent is dosed to the load and to Gurugram's hard water, with wash temperature set by fabric rather than by habit, and an extra rinse to clear residue.

Controlled drying

Dried at a temperature chosen for the fabric, so cotton does not shrink and synthetics do not glaze. This also avoids the dust and sun-fade that balcony drying leaves on darks, and the mildew smell clothes pick up when they never quite dry during the monsoon.

Fold, pack and deliver

Folded, checked back against the pickup count, packed as your order alone and delivered to your door across Gurugram.
