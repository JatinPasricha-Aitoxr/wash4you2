Linen is a popular fabric known for its breathable texture, natural appearance, and comfortable feel. From linen shirts and trousers to dresses, bedsheets, table linen, and other household fabrics, linen needs the right cleaning method to maintain its quality. Incorrect washing, excessive heat, or harsh detergents can cause shrinkage, stiffness, fading, or damage to the fabric.

## Why Does Linen Need Special Cleaning?

Linen is made from natural flax fibers. Although it is durable, improper cleaning can affect its texture and shape. Some linen garments may also have delicate embroidery, special finishes, or blended fabrics that require careful handling.

Professional linen cleaning considers the fabric type, colour, stains, and care instructions before selecting the appropriate cleaning process.

- Linen shirts and trousers

The appropriate cleaning method depends on the item's material, construction, colour, and care label.

## Linen Cleaning vs Washing at Home

Washing linen at home may seem simple, but the wrong temperature, detergent, washing cycle, or drying method can affect the fabric.

Professional cleaning can be particularly useful for expensive linen garments, heavily stained items, large household linen, and pieces with special detailing.

Before cleaning, always check the manufacturer's care label to determine whether the item is suitable for washing or requires professional cleaning.

## How Professional Linen Cleaning Works

The cleaning process generally involves several steps:

1. Fabric and care-label inspection
2. Identification of stains and problem areas
3. Selection of a suitable cleaning method
4. Careful cleaning and stain treatment
5. Drying according to fabric requirements
6. Finishing and quality inspection
7. Safe packaging for delivery

The exact process may vary depending on the type and condition of the linen item.

## How Often Should Linen Be Professionally Cleaned?

The cleaning frequency depends on how frequently the item is used. Everyday linen clothing may require cleaning after regular use, while curtains, table linen, and other household items may need less frequent professional cleaning.

Instead of following a fixed schedule, consider the fabric's condition, visible stains, odour, and manufacturer's care instructions.
