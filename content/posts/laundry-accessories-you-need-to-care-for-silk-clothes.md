Silk clothes feel elegant and luxurious, but they need more care than most fabrics. The right accessories make that care easier and protect your garments from the small mistakes that cause big damage. Here is a short, practical kit to build.

## Mild, pH-neutral detergent

Regular detergents can be too harsh for silk. Choose a gentle detergent made for delicates, with no bleach or strong enzymes. You need only a few drops per wash.

## Mesh laundry bags

A fine mesh bag protects silk from rubbing and snagging if you ever use a machine, and keeps small items like scarves from tangling. Use the gentlest cold cycle only when the care label allows it.

## A clean basin

A dedicated plastic basin for hand washing keeps delicates away from dirt and detergent residue left in buckets used for heavier clothes.

## White towels

Lay a plain white towel flat to press water out of silk without wringing. White avoids any chance of dye transfer.

## A flat drying rack

A mesh or flat drying rack lets silk dry evenly in the shade without stretching. It is especially useful for sarees, scarves and blouses.

## Padded or wide hangers

Thin wire hangers leave marks and pull shoulders out of shape. Padded or wide wooden hangers support the fabric properly.

## A garment steamer

A handheld steamer relaxes wrinkles without pressing a hot plate onto the fabric. It is gentler than an iron and works well on silk.

## A soft pressing cloth

A thin cotton cloth between the iron and the silk prevents shine marks and scorching.

## Breathable garment covers and cotton bags

For storage, choose cotton or muslin covers rather than plastic, which traps moisture and can lead to mildew and yellowing.

## What to leave out of the kit

- Chlorine bleach
- Strong stain removers and spot cleaners
- Hot irons without a pressing cloth
- Mothballs placed directly against fabric

## Quick checklist

- Gentle detergent, mesh bag, basin
- White towels and a flat rack
- Padded hangers, steamer, pressing cloth
- Cotton covers for storage
