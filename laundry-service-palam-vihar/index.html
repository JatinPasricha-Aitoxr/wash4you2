A township of houses, not towers

Palam Vihar sits at the far west end of Gurugram, and it was planned as a self-contained township long before the glass towers went up on the other side of the city. That history shows in the housing. Instead of thirty-storey blocks with a common lobby, you get lettered blocks of plotted houses, builder floors stacked two and three high, kothis with a front gate and a small lawn, and only a handful of apartment complexes mixed in. Families here tend to stay put. It is common to find grandparents, working parents and school-going children under one roof, with a household that has been in the same address for a decade or more.

That housing profile changes what laundry actually means. In a one-bedroom rental, laundry is a bag of office shirts. In a Palam Vihar house it is four bedrooms of bedsheets, two sets of curtains per room, a drawing-room sofa that gets used every single evening, and a winter cupboard full of blankets and razais that have to come out in November and go back in March. Most of that never fits in a domestic washing machine — not because the machine is bad, but because a 6 or 7 kg drum cannot move a double-bed blanket or a full-drop curtain enough to actually clean it. It comes out damp in the middle, smelling of the wash and nothing else, and takes two days on the terrace to dry. In monsoon or in a foggy December week, it does not dry at all.

What we take on most often here

Household textiles are the bulk of what we collect from this side of Gurugram. Long curtains that have gone dull from west-facing sun and construction dust. Fabric sofa upholstery and cushion covers. Blankets, quilts and dohars at seasonal changeover. Bedsheets and towels in volume. Alongside that, the ordinary weekly things: school and office wear, steam ironing, and dry cleaning for suits, sarees and lehengas when there is a wedding in the family — and in Palam Vihar there usually is.

How pickup works on a plotted street

Palam Vihar is a doorbell locality, not a guard-desk locality. There is no tower reception to leave a bag with, which is actually an advantage: you are standing right there when we count your items, so the count is agreed at the gate rather than argued about later. Your order is bagged separately from every other customer's, and you get a written receipt before we leave.

Two practical notes. First, block-and-house-number addresses confuse map apps, so our rider will call before arriving — please pick up. Second, if curtains are part of the pickup, take them off the rods before the slot rather than during it; hooks and rings are easier to remove at your own pace.
