Palam Vihar is a plotted township, and that changes what the dry cleaning pile looks like. In a flat, curtains are a couple of windows. In an independent house across three floors, it's the drawing room drapes, the stairwell panels, the terrace-side sheers and the heavy winter blackouts that come down every March — plus the sofa covers, the quilts that were stored in the loft, and the good bedsheets kept for guests. Most of it is too big for a domestic machine, too heavy to carry, and too easy to ruin by washing at home. Cotton and linen drapes shrink and never hang straight again. Velvet flattens. Blanket bindings pucker.

So on this side of Gurugram we treat dry cleaning as two jobs in one visit. The household side: curtains, sofa and cushion covers, blankets, dohars, dhurries — solvent-cleaned or wet-cleaned depending on what the fabric and the lining actually are, checked before anything starts. And the wardrobe side that runs alongside it, especially through wedding and festival months: suits, sarees, lehengas, sherwanis, woollens, and embellished pieces where the zari, sequins and dupatta borders need hand attention rather than a machine cycle.

The logistics here are house-to-house, not lobby-to-lobby. There's no single reception desk collecting for a whole tower, so our rider comes to your gate. A curtain order is counted with you at the door — rod by rod, room by room, hooks and rings noted — and you get a written receipt before anything leaves. Your order is packed and processed separately from every other customer's, which matters when six households send in near-identical white sheers the same week. Pickup and delivery across Gurugram, including Palam Vihar, are free.

Nazuk kapde, extra care — the delicate things get handled by hand.
